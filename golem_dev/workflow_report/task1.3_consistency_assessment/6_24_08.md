

## Versioning solution

Incrementing to a **Major** version is logically required because changing an ontology's formal semantics (restrictions and axioms)—even while preserving the existing vocabulary names—alters its logical entailment and breaks backward compatibility (see [Semantic Versioning 2.0.0](https://semver.org/) and [W3C Member Submission](https://www.w3.org/submissions/2024/SUBM-semantic-versioning-20241127/)). Version compatibility fundamentally means that an importing system will not be harmed by switching versions; altering axioms risks introducing semantic contradictions or untyped resources into active data pipelines.

## Issues Concerning the Adoption of DOLCE-Lite's Generic-Dependence Property

GOLEM reuses DOLCE-Lite's property of **generic dependence**. The property currently contains the following two example declarations:

```turtle
dol:generically-dependent-on
    skos:example
        "England in the Harry Potter series (G12) generically dependent on England in the real world (geographical-place)." ,
        "The romantic love between Ron and Hermione (G4) generically dependent on their kiss in the chamber of secrets (G5)." .
```

### Example 1: England (Fictional) and England (Real World)

The first example may be an appropriate instance of **generic dependence**: the fictional representation of England in the *Harry Potter* series depends on *some* entity satisfying "England-in-the-real-world"-like properties. However, it is not clear that "England-in-the-real-world" actually refers to a type rather than to a specific instance.

It seems instead that we are referring to a specific configuration of England as historically determined in the actual world. Grammatically, the term "England" is a proper name, and as such its referent should be considered a particular entity, not a type.

If the reasoning above is sound, the ontological dependence we are dealing with is that of **specific dependence**. Furthermore, once the fictional England is created, it only needs the real referent to have existed at or before creation. This second consideration allows us to further qualify the dependence as **historical**. That being said, the relation we need is that of **specific historical dependence**.

### Example 2: The Ron–Hermione Relationship and Their Kiss

With regard to the second example, the same *ratio* applies straightforwardly. The romantic relationship between Ron and Hermione is presented as depending on the occurrence of a particular event, namely their kiss in the Chamber of Secrets. This is not naturally characterized as generic dependence. Rather, it is again a case of **specific historical dependence**, since the existence of the particular social relationship is represented as depending on the occurrence of a particular event in the storyline.

### Proposed Solution

A viable solution could be to replace the use of `dol:generically-dependent-on` with the property `historicallyDependsOn` from the gUFO lightweight top-level ontology.

The property `gufo:historicallyDependsOn` is appropriate because its intended semantics explicitly cover dependence between individual events or individual objects, in which the dependent entity's existance depends on at least one occurence (previous or at the same time) of another entity. Moreover, historical dependence is not restricted to strict causation: the relation can also capture cases in which an event is historically dependent on another event without the latter being by itself sufficient to cause the former. The property can therefore represent the intended relation without introducing a new GOLEM-specific property.

The proposed declaration, which would implement a lightweight import of a single gUFO construct, is the following:

```turtle
@prefix gufo: <https://nemo-ufes.github.io/gufo#> .

gufo:historicallyDependsOn
    a owl:ObjectProperty ,
      owl:TransitiveProperty ;
    rdfs:domain dol:particular ;
    rdfs:range dol:particular ;
    rdfs:comment "..."@en ;
    skos:example "..."@en ;
    rdfs:seeAlso gufo:historicallyDependsOn .
```

### Application to Example 1

The definition we give for `G12_Narrative_Location` is as follows:

```turtle
golem:G12_Narrative_Location rdf:type owl:Class ;
    rdfs:subClassOf dlp_extDnS:non-agentive-social-object ;
    rdfs:comment "A narrative location is the spatial context in which events unfold—serving as the immediate frame of action within a story. It is a non-agentive social object shaped by the narrative structure. While it may derive features from real-world locations, it exists solely as an entity within the narrative domain."@en ;
    skos:example "'England' or the 'Forbidden Forest' as depicted in the Harry Potter series—both conceived and situated within the fictional narrative context." .
```

To guide users toward the appropriate property when modeling narrative locations based on real-world locations, I would add the following statement to the class:


```turtle
skos:scopeNote "When a narrative location is explicitly grounded in a real-world counterpart (e.g. 'England'), gufo:historicallyDependsOn may optionally be asserted to link the instance to the corresponding real-world geographical-place. This is not required: purely fictional locations (e.g. 'the Forbidden Forest') need no such assertion."@en .
```


### Application to Example 2

The current GOLEM modeling of `G4_Social_Relationship` uses the DOLCE property `dol:generically-dependent-on` to express a dependence between a social relationship and a `G5_Narrative_Event`:

```turtle
golem:G4_Social_Relationship
    rdfs:subClassOf
        [ a owl:Restriction ;
          owl:onProperty dol:generically-dependent-on ;
          owl:someValuesFrom golem:G5_Narrative_Event
        ] .
```

This does not correspond to the intended interpretation of the Ron–Hermione example. The intended claim is instead that the social relationship is **specifically historically dependent** on a narrative event that contributes to bringing about or establishing the relationship. Therefore, the enforcing constraint would be as follows:

```turtle
golem:G4_Social_Relationship
    rdfs:subClassOf
        [ a owl:Restriction ;
          owl:onProperty gufo:historicallyDependsOn ;
          owl:someValuesFrom golem:G5_Narrative_Event
        ] .
```
