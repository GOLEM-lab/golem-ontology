

## Background

Following the achievement of internal consistency requirements for the GOLEM T-Box, a new issue emerged concerning the excessive execution time of automated reasoning. The main cause was identified in the size of GOLEM's import closure, which currently amounts to **17,308 axioms**.

Further investigation revealed two additional architectural issues:

1. The mapping between **Erlangen CRM (ECRM)** and **CIDOC CRM 7.1.3** is implemented through `owl:sameAs` rather than `owl:equivalentClass` and `owl:equivalentProperty`.

2. **LRMoo** imports **CRM OWL**, an OWL implementation of CIDOC CRM that is distinct from Erlangen CRM.

As a consequence, the LRMoo classes and properties used in GOLEM become disconnected from the rest of the ontology architecture based on Erlangen CRM. Since CRM OWL is not formally aligned with ECRM in a way that would allow transitive inference of the relevant mappings, LRMoo entities remain tied to a separate semantic framework.

---

## Research Questions

Three questions were identified as critical for determining a suitable architectural solution:

1. Is it feasible to replace LRMoo with Erlangen FRBRoo (EFRBRoo) and move entirely to the Erlangen ecosystem (`ECRM + EFRBRoo`)?

2. Is it possible to remove DUL and align the GOLEM quality pattern directly to DLP?

3. Why is the mapping between ECRM and CIDOC CRM implemented using `owl:sameAs`?

---

## Investigation 1: Replacing LRMoo with EFRBRoo

### Versioning Issues

While Erlangen developers generally recommend using versioned namespaces whenever automatic reasoning and identifier stability are important, this option is not consistently available for EFRBRoo.

The versioned EFRBRoo ontology imports a non-versioned version of ECRM. Clarification was requested from the Erlangen CRM developers, who explained that:

- EFRBRoo was developed before versioned namespaces became a common requirement.
- Maintaining multiple synchronized versions of ECRM and EFRBRoo would have imposed a significant maintenance burden.
- The ontology is relatively old and reflects historical modelling decisions.
- More recent developments are represented by LRMoo and its OWL serializations maintained by the CIDOC CRM community.

### Semantic and Engineering Issues

Even if migration were technically possible, it would not be straightforward.

EFRBRoo is based on **FRBRoo 1.0.2 (2012)** and differs substantially from LRMoo.

The GOLEM ontology currently relies on:

- `R3i_realises`
- `R5_has_component`

In LRMoo these properties connect the conceptual entities **Work** and **Expression**.

In EFRBRoo, however, the corresponding properties operate on more specialized entities such as:

- `F14 Individual Work`
- `F22 Self-Contained Expression`

These entities represent a lower level of abstraction and would require non-trivial remodelling to preserve the intended semantics currently implemented in GOLEM.

### Assessment

Migration from LRMoo to EFRBRoo appears possible but neither simple nor particularly advantageous. The semantic adaptations required would introduce additional complexity without providing clear benefits.

## Investigation 2: Removing DUL and Aligning to DLP

### Motivation

GOLEM currently imports DUL primarily to support the modelling of qualities.

However, DOLCE Lite—the foundational framework extended by the DLP modules already used in GOLEM—does not require the explicit instantiation of `Region` entities whenever `Quality` instances are introduced.

Furthermore, GOLEM does not currently implement the full DOLCE treatment of qualities based on trope theory.

### Experiment

A test configuration was created in which:

- DUL imports were removed.
- `golem:G2_Feature` was aligned to `dlp_lite:Quality`.
- `golem:GP0_has_feature` was aligned to `dlp_lite:has-quality`.

### Results

- GOLEM's axiom closure decreased from **17,308** to **15,643 axioms**.
- HermiT reasoning completed successfully.
- No consistency issues were detected.

### Assessment

The alignment to DLP appears both semantically acceptable and technically advantageous. Since GOLEM does not exploit the specific ontological machinery of DOLCE qualities, replacing DUL with DLP provides a lighter and more manageable solution.

## Investigation 3: The Use of `owl:sameAs` in ECRM

### Analysis

Erlangen CRM represents an OWL-DL reinterpretation of CIDOC CRM, whereas CIDOC CRM itself is distributed primarily as an RDFS ontology.

Using `owl:equivalentClass` or `owl:equivalentProperty` would imply a stronger extensional identity between the corresponding constructs.

Such equivalence is difficult to justify because ECRM introduces a richer OWL-DL semantics and additional modelling commitments that are absent in the original CIDOC CRM specification.

### Role of `owl:sameAs`

Although `owl:sameAs` is primarily intended for individuals, it can also be applied to classes and properties through OWL 2 DL punning.

Under punning:

- the same URI is interpreted simultaneously as a class/property and as an individual;
- identity can be asserted at the meta-level without collapsing the formal semantics of the corresponding ontology constructs.

This mechanism allows ECRM to maintain an intensional correspondence with CIDOC CRM while preserving the independence of the two formalizations.

### Assessment

The use of `owl:sameAs` appears to be a deliberate engineering choice aimed at signalling conceptual identity while avoiding stronger equivalence commitments that could arise from `owl:equivalentClass` and `owl:equivalentProperty`.

## Alternative Architecture: LRMoo + CRM OWL + DLP

One possible solution is to accept the CRM OWL ecosystem and use:

- LRMoo
- CRM OWL
- DLP

instead of relying on Erlangen CRM.

However, CRM OWL presents some drawbacks:

- approximately **6,760 axioms**;
- a larger axiomatisation than ECRM;
- a less rigorous OWL formalization despite the higher axiom count.

---

## Lightweight Import Strategy for LRMoo

An alternative approach was tested to preserve the use of ECRM while avoiding the import overhead introduced by LRMoo and CRM OWL.

### Method

The direct import of LRMoo was removed.

Instead:

1. Only the required LRMoo entities were redeclared locally in GOLEM.
2. The original LRMoo namespace was preserved.
3. The relevant subclass and subproperty relations were recreated manually.
4. These relations were aligned directly to ECRM rather than CRM OWL.

Because the only axioms associated with the required LRMoo entities are simple subclass and subproperty declarations, reproducing them locally does not alter reasoning behaviour.
The lightweight reuse strategy preserves the semantics required by GOLEM while substantially reducing the ontology import closure and reasoning costs. Since GOLEM adopts a versioned ontology strategy, semantic reproducibility is guaranteed independently of future developments of LRMoo. For the selected version, the LRMoo entities used by GOLEM are characterized only by simple subclass and subproperty relations, which can be reproduced locally against ECRM without affecting reasoning outcomes. The approach therefore provides a computationally efficient alternative to importing the complete LRMoo and CRM OWL dependency chain.

### Results

This strategy removes dependencies on:

- most of LRMoo (~690 axioms);
- CRM OWL (~6,760 axioms);
- DUL (~1,555 axioms).

The overall GOLEM import closure decreases to:

**8,215 axioms**

### Reasoning Performance

Using a local configuration limited to:

- 3 GB RAM
- 2 CPU cores

HermiT completed classification successfully in approximately:

**13 minutes**

---

## Conclusion

The investigations suggest that the most promising architecture is based on:

- Erlangen CRM (ECRM)
- DLP
- lightweight referential reuse of selected LRMoo constructs

This approach:

- preserves the intended semantics of GOLEM;
- avoids dependence on CRM OWL;
- eliminates unnecessary imports from DUL;
- reduces the ontology closure from 17,308 to 8,215 axioms;
- enables successful reasoning within reasonable computational limits.
